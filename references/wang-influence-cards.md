# Wang — Price of Influence (operator cards)

Bundled distillation from Claire Wang, *The Price of Influence*. Skills load this file; do not require a live Literature Note for the core run.

## Four stages

1. **Know your buyer** — You do not need everyone. Move the person whose yes actually matters. Ask: Who decides? What do they value? What makes them hesitate?
2. **Enter their world** — Assume you know nothing. Map what they value, how they see the problem, and the constraints they work under. Include influencers and people who must carry the idea forward — not only the wallet.
3. **Make your move** — Lead with what they need to hear in the first 60 seconds, in language they recognise. Destination before route. Three rapid checks on their side: Is this relevant to me? Can I afford to ignore this? Is this worth my time?
4. **Become the default** — Deliver, then deliver again. Trust accumulates across yeses. Reliability beats a single clever pitch.

Core: understand what the buyer values, then deliver it in a way they recognise (alignment in value).

## Authority trap

- Authority gets **compliance**, not **commitment**.
- Stop leading with title (big or small). If you have authority, assume people will not follow until you earn it. If you lack it, assume you can still lead.
- Do not trust a verbal yes. Look for engagement: follow-up questions, ownership of next steps, movement between meetings.
- Pushback and challenges often signal engagement, not disagreement. Pleasant passive "yes… we'll see" closes conversation.
- Watch for **silence**, not only resistance. Silence can mean they already checked out.

## Pain / Cost / Gain

Short form — full coaching card: `references/pain-cost-gain-card.md` (`ci-pcg`, `ci-mirror`).

Before pitching, check whether the buyer **feels** the problem:

- **Pain** — Do they feel the problem now, or is it still abstract / our problem?
- **Cost** — What does leaving it unresolved cost them (time, risk, status, money)?
- **Gain** — What desired outcome are they really buying? (They buy the promise of an outcome, not our feature list.)

Vitamin vs painkiller: if the felt problem is invisible, hold up a mirror — do not push more evidence. Structural rejection (wrong buyer / no urgency / value mismatch) will not be fixed by sweetening the offer.

## Headline–Insights–CTA

Short form — full coaching card: `references/headline-insights-cta-card.md` (`ci-hic`, `ci-frame`).

In the first 60 seconds, speak their language:

1. **Headline** — The outcome / stake in their words.
2. **Insights** — The few facts that make the headline credible (not your full discovery journey).
3. **CTA** — The next yes you actually need.

Common failure: replaying your analytical path instead of showing the destination first. Highlighting value that does not matter to the buyer loses even when the idea is smart. CTA first when thinking; last when speaking.

## Become the default

Short form — full card: `references/become-default-card.md` (`ci-default`).

Influence is an invitation, not a power move. Default status is earned by repeated delivery and reliability after the first yes. Optimise for a clear Open Ask; compound Default Status Notes across sessions on the same Akte.
